# cr11-2: periodic custody verification serializes the ingest writer

**Severity**: HIGH — each full integrity scan blocks every ingest commit and
retention mutation for a duration proportional to retained evidence.
**Status**: In progress
**Branch**: —
**Commit**: `fe4378e2cedcc8266d3cdfec3c43e60bc0af669a`

## Evidence

`SqliteIngestStore.VerifyCustodyAsync` acquired `_writerGate` and ran the
entire custody, subject, and tombstone scan through `_writer`. The periodic
witness service invokes that scan every configured cadence, 60 seconds by
default.

## Predicted observable failure

As the retained corpus grows, every periodic scan creates a matching ingest
latency stall. Producers can time out and retry while the receiver is merely
performing its own health check.

## What

An exhaustive read-only audit unnecessarily occupied the single serialized
writer connection and gate.

## Approach

Retain the exhaustive periodic integrity semantics, but run each scan through
a private read-only SQLite connection inside one explicit WAL read
transaction. That pins a coherent snapshot across all verification queries
while allowing the writer to commit concurrently.

## Guard proof

`SqliteIngestStoreTests::Periodic_custody_scan_does_not_hold_the_ingest_writer_gate`
pins the scan snapshot after its first subject read, blocks it before the
second, and concurrently commits an event. Before the repair, the commit did
not complete within one second because verification held `_writerGate`;
after the repair, the commit completes while the scan is held and the scan
still verifies its coherent pre-commit snapshot as healthy.

## Coder dispute (if any)

None; the writer serialization was independently reproduced. The repair does
not adopt the reviewer's suggested incremental-only audit because S4b
requires periodic detection of mutation to previously witnessed evidence.

## Known gaps

The exhaustive scan still consumes read I/O and CPU by design, but no longer
holds the ingest writer gate.
