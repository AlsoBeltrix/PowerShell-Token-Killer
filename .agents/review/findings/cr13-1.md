# cr13-1: witness and anchor parent gate overstated

**Severity**: LOW — operators could rely on a nonexistent availability guard or
apply owner-only permissions to shared system parents.
**Status**: Verified
**Branch**: —
**Commit**: `45e4ede48950e3aeb1318af222519a7b2ed31d7f`

## Evidence

`siem/PtkSiemReceiver/README.md` said the witness and anchor directories and
their immediate parents must satisfy the full platform ownership/mode/ACL
policy and that insecure objects fail closed. File paths do verify their
containing directory in `Security/SiemProtectedPath.cs`, but
`RetainExternalDirectoryCore` and `VerifyExternalDirectoryCore` verify the
witness/anchor directory itself and refuse linked lexical components without
applying the platform policy to its parent. The recommended `/var/lib`, `/mnt`,
`/Volumes`, and drive-root layouts also cannot make those shared parents owned
only by the receiver identity.

## Predicted observable failure

A witness or anchor directory under a parent writable by another principal
starts successfully despite the documented fail-closed claim. That principal
can rename or delete the protected directory and disrupt custody-history
availability. A literal operator could instead try to make a shared system
parent receiver-owned and owner-only, disrupting unrelated services.

## What

The S7 operator guide overstated which immediate parents the protected-path
startup gate verifies.

## Approach

Describe the implemented boundary exactly: protected files and their immediate
containing directories satisfy platform policy; witness and anchor directories
themselves satisfy it; their parents receive lexical redirect checks but not
ownership/mode/ACL verification. Make protection of those parents an explicit
provisioning responsibility.
