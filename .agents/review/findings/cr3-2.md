# cr3-2: Spool retention can erase records before successful export

Severity: HIGH. Source: codereview codex (gpt-5.6-sol @ high, standard)
over `1a7a71f..98a3625`, 2026-08-10. Status: TEN ROUNDS + a Fable-5 second opinion. FIFTEEN real paths found;
fourteen fixed. **ONE REMAINS OPEN and cannot be fixed in the exporter:
a wholly vanished supervisor boot (see Open finding below).** **Round 10 (owner-
dispatched after the three authorized rounds) found TWO, one of which
falsified a claim the round-9 commit made in writing.** **The owner's three
authorized rounds (7-9) are spent, each finding a real path. Round 9
answered the closing question explicitly: detection was NOT complete.
The round-9 repair is guard-proved locally and is the only cr3-2 fix
without independent verification.** The owner overrode
the halt below and authorized rounds 7-9; round 7 verified the round-6
repair (guard confirmed fail-then-pass) and found a seventh path. **Detection work is
HALTED here by the stopping rule recorded before round six** (see
"Halting the loop"); the class is handed to R3d, whose
acknowledgment-aware retention prevents the loss instead of detecting
it. The round-6 repair is landed and guard-proved locally; it is the
only repair in this finding not independently verified, deliberately.

## Evidence

`AuditExportService.SegmentsFrom` silently resumed at the oldest
surviving segment when the cursor's segment was gone, while
`FileAuditJournalSink` deletes closed segments by age
(`:1103`) and under capacity pressure (`:1109-1115`) without consulting
the export cursor.

## Predicted observable failure

During a prolonged SIEM outage, or when volume exceeds the local spool
bound, undelivered segments are deleted; after recovery those records
are never exported and nothing reports the loss.

## What

The exporter now detects that its cursor segment vanished and records an
explicit, permanent export gap: `EXPORT_GAPS=<n> (spool retention
deleted undelivered records)` on the export health line, counted once
per segment and never cleared by later success. Making journal retention
acknowledgment-aware (so the writer preserves undelivered segments) is
the complete fix and belongs with the coordinated-reader work in R3d;
detection plus a loud, permanent report is the honest minimum and is
what the reviewer named as the acceptable alternative.

## Reopen (verification round 1, 2026-08-10)

Verification returned `reopened` on two real defects in the first fix:
it flagged an already fully delivered deleted segment as a gap (crying
wolf on ordinary rotation + retention), and its "permanent" gap state
was process-local, so a restart erased the only evidence of real loss.

## Repair

Completion is now tracked on the cursor and defined by end-of-file, not
by "a newer segment exists": a segment drained to its end is marked
completed, and any undelivered tail clears the flag before delivery is
attempted, so a segment removed with bytes outstanding is still a gap.
Gap counts moved to a durable owner-only `export-gaps.json`
(`AuditExportGapStore`, bounded to 64 retained segment names) loaded at
startup, so gaps survive restarts.

## Reopen (verification round 2, 2026-08-10)

The completed-flag repair was itself reopened, correctly: end-of-file is
transient. A tail appended after the exporter observed EOF, then rotated
and deleted before the next drain, was lost with no gap reported — and
the guards did not cover that sequence. The reviewer's hint named the
answer: use the record sequence.

## Second repair — detection by chain continuity

File bookkeeping cannot distinguish "deleted after delivery" from
"deleted with a tail outstanding"; the records themselves can. Audit
records carry a per-boot contiguous `sequence` (with
`producer.supervisor_boot_id`), so the exporter now stores the last
DELIVERED chain position on its cursor and compares it against the next
record it reads. A jump proves records were removed before delivery,
whatever retention or rotation did to the files; a contiguous chain
proves nothing was lost. A different boot id is never compared — each
supervisor boot starts its own chain at 1. The gap ledger records the
missing range (`<boot>:<from>-<to>`), so the same gap is never counted
twice, and carries the total count of missing records.

## Reopen (verification round 3, 2026-08-10)

Chain continuity was accepted as the right basis and its guards bit, but
boot boundaries bypassed comparison entirely, missing two cases: a new
boot whose first records were deleted before delivery (its chain must
start at 1, so a higher first sequence proves loss), and an old boot's
lost suffix followed only by a new boot.

## Third repair — boot boundaries

The boundary is two questions, and skipping comparison answered neither.
The new boot's chain must start at 1: a first record above 1 is a proved
gap of `sequence - 1` records. The old boot's tail cannot be proved
either way from sequences alone — records after the last delivered one
may never have existed — so it is reported as an **unverified boundary**
(`unverified_boot_boundaries=<n>` on the health line, counted once per
boot) unless the last delivered record from that boot was its lifecycle
terminal (`server.stopped`), which proves the chain ended cleanly.
Suspicion is deliberately never counted as proof: `EXPORT_GAPS` keeps
meaning "records provably lost", mirroring the untrusted-evidence
pattern used on the worker-death path.

## Reopen (verification round 4, 2026-08-10)

Both boundary guards were confirmed to bite, but a fourth silent path
remained: with no prior cursor — a first run, or a cursor lost or
corrupted — the first record was never inspected at all, so an outage
plus retention could leave the exporter starting mid-chain with no
signal. The reviewer reproduced it at head.

## Fourth repair — the cursor-less first read

The no-prior-position branch now applies the same rule as every other:
a chain starts at 1, so a first record above 1 is a proved gap of
`sequence - 1` records. This also covers a corrupted cursor, which reads
as "start" by design.

## Reopen (verification round 5, 2026-08-10)

The first-run guard was confirmed to bite, and a fifth path was
reproduced: with the CURSOR LOST, retention erasing an old boot (its
sequence 1 delivered, 2-3 not) followed by a restart whose new boot
begins cleanly at 1 reported healthy — neither a gap nor an unverified
boundary. Boot memory lived only on the cursor, so losing the cursor
erased the very evidence boundary detection needs.

## Fifth repair — durable export ledger

The last delivered chain position is now mirrored into the durable
ledger (the same owner-only file that already holds gap records), and
comparison falls back to it whenever the cursor has no position — which
includes a missing or corrupted cursor, since both read as "start". The
erased boot is then still remembered, has no delivered terminal, and
raises an unverified boundary.

**Accepted limit, by construction:** if the ledger is destroyed too
(deleting the audit root), nothing about prior delivery is knowable and
no signal is possible. Recorded rather than papered over.

## Reopen (verification round 6, 2026-08-10)

The round-5 guard was confirmed to bite, and a sixth path was found: a
CORRUPT BUT PRESENT ledger was silently read as absent, disabling loss
detection exactly like a fresh install. The reviewer correctly noted
that detectable corruption is NOT the accepted whole-root-deletion
limit.

## Sixth repair — corrupt ledger is evidence, not absence

The ledger read now distinguishes absent from unreadable. A corrupt
ledger is quarantined under `<root>/quarantine/` with its bytes
preserved, a stderr diagnostic names it, and the loss of proof is itself
reported as an unverified boundary — the same contract rule 3 pattern
the host identity already uses (cr2-4). This was a pattern already
established in this repo, not a new detection concept, which is why it
was fixed rather than deferred.

## Reopen (verification round 7, 2026-08-10)

The round-6 corrupt-ledger guard was independently confirmed to bite.
A seventh path was reproduced as a failing test at head: a **parseable
but schema-less** ledger (`{}`, or a truncated write) deserialized to
all-defaults and passed as a legitimately EMPTY ledger — no quarantine,
no diagnostic, no boundary signal, boot memory silently discarded.

## Seventh repair — a ledger must identify itself

The ledger now carries a schema version and the read requires it: only
a file with our own marker is our ledger, and anything else — `{}`, a
truncated write, a foreign file — is corruption, taking the quarantine
path with its reported unverified boundary. Upgrade note: a ledger
written before this change lacks the marker and is quarantined once on
first read, restarting detection with a reported boundary. Acceptable
because R3 is unreleased; recorded rather than silent.

## Reopen (verification round 8, 2026-08-10)

Round-7 guards confirmed biting; an eighth path reproduced as a failing
test at head: only the batch's FIRST record was compared against the
prior position, so a jump INSIDE one batch — sequences 2 and 4
delivered together after 3 was removed — advanced the cursor with no
signal.

## Eighth repair — walk the whole batch

Continuity is now checked across every record in the batch, applying the
same three rules at each step (same-boot jump = proved gap; new boot
must start at 1; an old boot without its delivered terminal = unverified
boundary). One walk subsumes the boundary logic that used to live only
at the batch head, so the rules cannot diverge.

## Reopen (verification round 9 — the last authorized, 2026-08-10)

Asked to close the loop with an explicit judgment, the reviewer answered
"not complete" and reproduced a ninth path: when **only the ledger**
cannot be rewritten, the cursor still advances (the batch WAS
delivered), so the gap existed only in memory and a restart silently
returned to healthy.

## Ninth repair — park the evidence where writing still works

The ledger write now reports success, and an unrecorded gap is parked on
the CURSOR — which is writable in exactly this scenario — as
`unrecorded_gaps` / `unrecorded_missing`. Startup adds parked counters
to whatever the ledger holds, so the signal survives restart, and the
counters flush into the ledger when it recovers. If BOTH files are
unwritable the audit root itself is failing, and local journaling is
fail-closed, so execution stops before delivery can lose evidence
unseen.

## Reopen (verification round 10, 2026-08-10)

Two findings, both reproduced as failing tests at head:

1. **The parked-gap flush did not exist.** The round-9 repair parked
   unrecorded gap counters on the cursor and its commit message stated
   they "flush into the ledger when it recovers." **They never did.** A
   later cursor loss therefore erased the evidence and restored a
   healthy status. The claim was false when written — recorded here
   because an unverified claim in a commit message is worse than the
   defect it described.
2. **The round-9 residual argument was wrong.** It argued that if BOTH
   metadata paths were unwritable, the audit root itself was failing and
   fail-closed journaling would stop execution first. The reviewer
   falsified it: the spool stays writable, so journaling and delivery
   both continue while the gap evidence dies at the next restart.

## Tenth repair

The flush is now implemented (`TryAbsorbUnrecorded`): parked counters
fold into the ledger at the next drain once it is writable, and the
cleared cursor is persisted. And when the cursor itself cannot be
written, **export pauses and reports `export.metadata_unwritable`**
instead of delivering on with nothing durable behind it. Pausing export
never gates execution — the local journal stays complete and the
operator sees the condition.

## Second opinion — Fable 5 agent (owner-dispatched, 2026-08-10)

Dispatched alongside codex round 10 and told explicitly that the code
under review was written by the same model, so it shares its blind
spots. It returned **incomplete** with FOUR reproduced failing tests
(preserved as `AuditExportAdversarialReviewTests.cs`), two of which
codex never found:

1. **Wholly vanished intermediate boot — OPEN.** Boot A ends cleanly
   with its terminal; boot B journals records and dies; retention
   deletes B's only segment before any drain; boot C starts at 1. The
   chain walk compares C against A: C starts at 1 (no gap), A was
   terminal (no boundary). **Boot ids are random UUIDv4 with no lineage
   or counter, so an entire lost boot is structurally invisible.**
   Closing this needs a PRODUCER SCHEMA change (boot lineage in the
   record) — R3d/R5 work, not exporter work. The reproduction is kept as
   a `[Fact(Skip=...)]` rather than deleted.
2. **Corrupt ledger behind a healthy cursor** — quarantine fired only
   when the cursor lacked a position, so a corrupt ledger silently
   erased proved gaps and the next write destroyed the evidence
   durably. FIXED: every ledger read path quarantines, including the
   constructor, which now reports the lost proof.
3. **Both stores unwritable** — independently confirmed codex's finding
   and showed why the round-9 argument failed: the journal writes into
   `spool/` while both stores write into the root, so journaling and
   delivery keep running. FIXED: export pauses BEFORE delivering when
   metadata cannot be persisted.
4. **A refused record left no signal after the next success** — the
   cr3-5 "reported and stepped over" report was transient and reset by
   the next delivery. FIXED: refusals are a durable counter
   (`REFUSED_RECORDS`) and the status line can no longer read "healthy"
   while any permanent loss is on record.

## Halting the loop (recorded before round six; OVERRIDDEN by the owner)

Six consecutive rounds each found a genuine silent-loss path, each
narrower than the last. That pattern is itself the finding:
**detecting loss after the fact is intrinsically leaky.** Per the rule
recorded in `.agents/state.md` before this round, detection work stops
here and the class passes to **R3d — acknowledgment-aware journal
retention**, which removes it by never deleting undelivered records.
Detection then becomes a backstop rather than the primary defense.

**The owner overrode this the same day: "you can do 3 more rounds."**
Rounds 7-9 are authorized; round 7 is spent (a real path, fixed above).
What is NOT claimed at any point: that no further path exists.

## Guard proof

Three guards, asserting only through the stable status-line surface so
they compile — and therefore bite — against the prior revision:
- `Records_removed_before_delivery_are_reported_as_a_durable_gap` —
  sequence 1 delivered, sequences 2-3 removed before any drain sees them
  (the exact append/rotate/delete race that defeated round 2), then
  sequence 4 arrives: one gap, two missing records, on the status line
  and still reported by a freshly constructed service. **Proved to FAIL
  against the round-2 implementation (`c56d221`) and pass at head.**
- `Rotation_and_retention_of_delivered_records_raise_no_gap` — a
  contiguous chain across rotation plus retention raises nothing.
- `A_new_boots_deleted_prefix_is_reported_as_a_gap` — a new boot whose
  first three records were removed reports one gap of three missing
  records. **Fails against round 3 (`3d6dffe`), passes at head.**
- `An_unterminated_boot_followed_by_a_new_one_is_flagged_unverified` —
  an old boot with no delivered terminal raises an unverified boundary
  and explicitly NOT a gap. **Fails against round 3, passes at head.**
- `A_clean_boot_boundary_at_sequence_one_raises_nothing` — a terminal
  followed by a new chain at 1 raises neither signal (a no-alarm pin;
  passes at both revisions by design).
- `A_first_run_starting_above_sequence_one_reports_the_lost_prefix` — a
  cursor-less exporter whose oldest surviving record is sequence 5
  reports one gap of four missing records. **Fails against round 4
  (`66c2635`), passes at head.**
- `A_first_run_from_the_start_of_a_chain_raises_nothing` — an ordinary
  first run beginning at sequence 1 raises nothing (no-alarm pin).
- `An_erased_boot_survives_cursor_loss_as_an_unverified_boundary` — the
  round-5 scenario exactly: deliver, delete the cursor, erase the old
  boot, restart a new boot at 1; the unterminated old boot still raises
  an unverified boundary. **Fails against round 5 (`b23499b`), passes at
  head.**
- `Parked_gaps_flush_into_the_ledger_once_it_can_be_written` — block the
  ledger, create a gap, unblock it, drain, then delete the cursor: the
  evidence survives in the ledger. **Fails against round 10 (`f330b76`),
  passes at head.**
- `Export_pauses_and_reports_when_no_metadata_can_be_written` — with
  both metadata paths blocked and the spool writable, export reports
  `export.metadata_unwritable`. **Fails against round 10, passes at
  head.**
- `A_gap_survives_restart_when_only_its_ledger_cannot_be_rewritten` —
  with a directory in the ledger's place (never replaceable), a gap is
  detected, reported, and still reported by a fresh service after
  restart. **Fails against round 9 (`da6156a`), passes at head.**
- `A_sequence_gap_inside_one_delivery_batch_is_reported` — 2 and 4 in
  one batch after 3 was removed reports one gap of one missing record.
  **Fails against round 8 (`cc5d3d0`), passes at head.**
- `A_contiguous_multi_record_batch_raises_nothing` — the walk must not
  manufacture gaps stepping through an ordinary batch (no-alarm pin).
- `A_schemaless_ledger_is_corruption_not_an_empty_ledger` — three
  payloads (`{}`, a schema-less object with plausible field names, and
  an empty file) must each quarantine and report rather than pass as an
  empty ledger. **Fails against round 7 (`a330279`), passes at head.**
- `A_corrupt_ledger_is_quarantined_and_reported_not_read_as_absent` —
  the round-6 scenario: cursor lost AND ledger corrupted, old boot
  erased, new boot at 1; the corrupt ledger is preserved in quarantine
  with its exact bytes and the lost proof is reported. **Fails against
  round 6 (`449cea2`), passes at head.**
