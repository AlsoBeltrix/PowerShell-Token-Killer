# r6x-2 #2 — review round blocked on transport, awaiting owner

**Raised**: 2026-07-25
**Kind of disagreement**: none — coder and reviewer do not disagree. This is a
**capability block**, routed to the owner because the remedy exceeds agent
authority.

## What happened

`codereview codex` was dispatched twice against `d6c9b22..9e4c618` (r6x-2 #2,
the test repair). Both rounds returned a schema-valid payload with matching
SHAs and `"verdict":"accepted"`, and both returned **`guard_confirmed: false`**.
The playbook computes acceptance in the orchestrator and fails closed on
`guard_confirmed` not being literally `true`, so **neither round is an
acceptance**.

Both failures were the same class — the reviewer's sandbox could not run this
repo's verification entry point:

1. Round 1, grant `--ignore-user-config -s workspace-write`: MSBuild/VSTest
   local IPC sockets denied; `dotnet test` could not start at all.
2. Round 2, fresh process, grant widened by exactly
   `-c sandbox_workspace_write.network_access=true`: `dotnet test` starts, but
   the real-apphost composition tests hit sandbox-denied `$HOME` fixture writes
   and apphost startup timeouts.

The playbook's step 4 makes a repeated capability denial terminal: record it,
report the transport unsupported, stop — do not search for a workaround. That
is why this stops here.

## Why the agent will not just fix it

The only remaining lever is `-s danger-full-access`, which removes the
reviewer's sandbox entirely. The playbook requires the reviewer's grant to be
"strictly narrower than the coder's", and widening it to full access is a
deliberate departure from that rule. It is the owner's call.

## What the owner must decide

- **(a) Grant the reviewer full access for review dispatches.** One launch-scoped
  flag, still headless and one-shot, nothing persistent edited. Restores what the
  playbook actually wants: the reviewer independently runs the guard proof rather
  than taking the coder's word. Cost: the reviewer runs unsandboxed on the
  owner's machine.
- **(b) Accept the rounds as unverified read-only review.** The reviewer read
  the diff and the finding and raised no substantive objection. Cheapest, but
  the second-party verification the loop exists to provide never happened, and
  the finding would have to say so.
- **(c) Dispatch a different harness** whose sandbox can spawn processes and
  write `$HOME`.

Recommended: **(a)**. The verification is the entire point of the round, the
grant is launch-scoped rather than persistent, and the coder already holds
strictly more access on this machine than the reviewer would gain.
